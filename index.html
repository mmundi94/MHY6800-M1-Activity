The CEO has one question: should Vonari create a dedicated product manager role, or should someone already on the team take on product decisions? Spend your week using what you learned in Module 1 to help answer it.

Company brief

Vonari fictional

Vonari, its people, and its numbers are fictional and were written for this practice. People are named by role only.

The company

  • About 30 people. Its app links families with private tutors.
  • The CEO and the CTO have made every product decision so far.

What's going wrong

  • 4 of the last 5 releases shipped late. Requests reach developers directly from sales, support, and both founders.
  • A Group Lessons feature took 3 developers 8 weeks. It was built because one large tutoring center asked. In its first two months, 5% of active families used it, and nobody checked who used it until a sales call.
  • Lesson reminders that never arrive caused 30% of support tickets last quarter. The fix has waited four months.
  • Nobody has spoken with families in six months.
  • The CEO and the CTO each keep their own list of top priorities.
  • Sales has promised two more custom reports to single tutoring centers.
  • Estimates for the reminder fix were off by three weeks, and nobody asked why.

What people say

  • CEO: "I'll spend the next six months raising funds. I want the final say on pricing."
  • CTO: "We have budget for one new role this year. I planned it as a developer."
  • Head of Support: "I hear from families every day."

The two options

Option A. Create a dedicated product manager role. Costs about the same as one senior developer, so the planned developer role waits. Hiring takes about three months, and a new person needs time to learn the product.
Option B. The Head of Support takes product decisions two days a week. No hiring cost or delay. Support work slows while the Head of Support spends time on product.
